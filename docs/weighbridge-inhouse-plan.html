01 · Short answer

Buy the weighing part. Build the software.

  • The quote has two separate systems. One is a certified weighing machine: steel platform, digital load cells, junction box and the IWT-186 weight indicator. The other is an automation layer, AWS 2.0: cameras that read number plates (ANPR), an RFID tag reader, a boom barrier, traffic lights, a hooter, and software that ties them together.
  • Don't try to make the weighing machine yourself. A weighbridge used to bill by the tonne must be an approved model under the Legal Metrology Act 2009, installed by a licensed maker and stamped by the Legal Metrology department. Your ₹93/MT contractor billing counts as trade. Buy this part from Essae or another approved maker.
  • Everything around it can be built in-house. Cameras, RFID, barriers and lights are standard off-the-shelf hardware. The software that runs them, and that links weighing to billing, Tally, the e-way bill and your owner dashboard, is where owning it pays off. I can write that software.
  • It will not save money up front. Essae's automation bundle at ₹6.9L is competitively priced. Doing it in-house costs about ₹5–11L more at the start, because you also pay for a server, a firewall, software work and a security test. What you get for that is control: your own data, your own rules against weighing fraud, links to your billing, and room to add more weighbridges or sites.
  • Timeline: about 15 weeks to go-live, 18 including the settling-in period. Civil works and stamping set the pace, not software. I can have the software ready and tested against simulated devices before the platform is even installed.
My recommendation: Option C below. Buy Essae's weighbridge, and buy their AWS hardware too if the price holds, but only on open terms: the indicator's data format, admin access to the cameras and RFID reader, and no lock-in to their software. Then run your own software on top. The quote's validity runs out around 2 October 2026, so raise these points before you place the order.

04 · Architecture

Target architecture: yard, control room, central system

Three tiers. Field devices talk only to the edge server in the control room, which weighs trucks even when the internet is down. The edge server connects outward to a central system at your head office or in an Indian cloud region. That central system holds the records, users, reports and every external integration.

09 · Security

Security: logins, data, integrations and fraud

A weighbridge is a cash register measured in tonnes. The main risks are an insider changing weights, a vehicle cheating the scale, and ransomware wiping the records. The controls below are ordered by those risks.

User login and access

  • One person, one account. No shared "operator" login. Accounts live in Keycloak.
  • MFA (authenticator app) for the owner, admins, accounts staff, supervisors, and every remote login. On-site operators use a password plus an MFA code, or an RFID staff card plus a PIN.
  • Passwords: at least 12 characters, checked against known-breached lists, stored as salted hashes. Lock the account for 15 minutes after 5 failed attempts.
  • Sessions: log out after 15 minutes idle on office screens. Operator sessions end with the shift.
  • Roles: Operator (weighs and prints, cannot edit). Supervisor (approves holds and corrections, and must give a reason). Accounts (billing and exports, cannot edit tickets). Owner/Admin (users, rates, masters). Auditor (read-only, including the audit log). Transporter portal (optional, sees only its own trucks).
  • Leavers are disabled the same day. Review everyone's access every quarter.
  • No AnyDesk or TeamViewer left running on weighbridge PCs. That is a common way in for attackers. Remote support goes through the VPN only.

Data security

  • In transit: TLS 1.2 or higher (1.3 preferred) on every link, internal ones included. The edge and central system use mutual TLS with a certificate per site.
  • At rest: full-disk encryption on the edge and servers. Databases on encrypted volumes. Photos encrypted in storage.
  • Tickets can't be changed once captured. A correction creates a new version with the reason and the approver. Each record and its photo hashes are chained with SHA-256, and the day's final hash is sent to the owner, so any later edit is detectable.
  • Backups (3-2-1): continuous database log shipping plus a nightly full backup, one copy on site and one encrypted offsite with a 30-day object lock so ransomware can't delete it. Test a restore every month.
  • Retention: keep tickets and bills for at least 72 months (the GST record rule), photos for 1–2 years, video for 30 days.
  • Personal data (DPDP Act 2023): drivers' names, mobile numbers and faces on camera are personal data. Put a notice at the gate, use the data only for weighing and billing, and delete it when the retention period ends.
  • Secrets are kept in a vault, never in code or on sticky notes.

Integration security

  • Devices: give each one a unique admin password, turn off cloud/P2P, UPnP and telnet, update firmware every quarter, and disable unused switch ports.
  • Protocols without logins (Modbus, LLRP, RS-232) are protected by isolation. The firewall only lets the edge server's IP reach them.
  • APIs: each integration gets its own key or OAuth2 client with the minimum scope, rotated every 90 days. Webhooks are signed (HMAC with a timestamp). Idempotency keys stop duplicate bills. Rate limits and input validation follow OWASP ASVS level 2 as the checklist.
  • Tally exposes port 9000 with no authentication, so allow only the connector host to reach it.
  • GSP, SMS and WhatsApp credentials go in the vault, with IP allow-listing where the provider supports it.
  • Logging: record every API call, login and admin action centrally, and keep it for 180 days. The CERT-In 2022 directions require 180-day log retention and reporting a cyber incident within 6 hours.

Weighing fraud controls

  • The weight comes only from the sealed indicator. Manual weight entry is switched off in the software.
  • Capture only when all of these hold: the reading is stable for 3 seconds or more, both IR beams are clear (the whole truck is on the platform), and the top camera shows nobody standing on it.
  • RFID and plate must match. Tags break when peeled off, so they can't be moved to another truck.
  • Tare checks: a fresh tare that differs from the vehicle's history by more than a set % triggers a hold. Force a re-tare every week.
  • Same truck twice within X minutes, or an odd hour gets flagged.
  • Remote-control or jammer cheating: a sealed junction box, a camera covering the pit and the box, a daily zero check, and a monthly test-weight check, all logged.
  • Owner's daily exception report on WhatsApp or email.

Physical and site

  • Lockable IP66 boxes on every pole, and surge protectors on every cable that runs out to the yard.
  • Air-conditioned control room with controlled entry and its own camera. The server and switch go in a locked rack.
  • An online UPS covering the edge server, network and indicator, plus a generator changeover.

Compliance and testing

  • Legal Metrology Act 2009: stamping at installation and periodic re-verification. Ask the Controller's office to confirm how the ticket software must show the certified weight.
  • DPDP Act 2023, CERT-In directions 2022, GST record-keeping.
  • CCTV: buy cameras that carry the STQC certification now mandatory in India.
  • Security test: a CERT-In empanelled auditor tests the system before go-live and then every year.

    • Truck types and maximum gross weight → platform length and capacity (likely 16–18 m, 80–100 t for loaded tippers).

  2. Site survey and layout week 1–2

    • Level ground, straight approach of at least one truck length on both sides, drainage, and where the control room goes (within 90 m of the poles, otherwise fibre).
    • Mark the pole positions for the ANPR cameras (3–5 m high), RFID antennas and booms. Plan conduits before the concrete is poured.

  3. Place the order on open terms week 2

    • Get quotes from Essae and two other approved makers. Get the indicator's output format, admin credentials for every device, and the foundation drawings in writing.

  4. Civil works and the control room weeks 2–8

    • Foundation and ramps to the maker's drawing. Allow 21–28 days for concrete to cure before installation.
    • Control room with AC, a locked rack, and earthing: chemical pits, separate for the weighbridge and the electronics, resistance measured and recorded.
    • HDPE conduits to every pole, pull cords left in.

  5. Software build, in parallel weeks 1–10

    • I write the edge service, the central system, the web app and the integrations, plus simulators for every device, so the whole flow is tested before any hardware arrives.
    • You review a working demo every week.

  6. Install and calibrate the weighbridge weeks 9–10

    • The maker's engineer installs it. You supply the crane, labour, welder and jacks. Budget beyond the 4 free days at ₹5,000/day.

  7. Legal Metrology stamping weeks 10–11

    • Book the inspector and arrange test weights. The certificate states the maximum capacity and scale interval, and the ticket must print them.

  8. Install automation hardware and the network weeks 8–12

    • Poles, cameras, RFID antennas and reader, booms, lights, IR beams, I/O panel and surge protectors.
    • Switch with VLANs, firewall rules as in the zone diagram, edge server with encrypted disks, NVR, UPS. Change every default password.

  9. Site integration and acceptance tests weeks 11–13

    • Connect each real device in place of its simulator. Test with a known truck and test weights.
    • Fault drills: pull the internet cable, pull a camera, cut the mains, try a wrong tag, stand someone on the platform, park one wheel off it.

  10. Enrol vehicles and users weeks 12–13

    • Fix tags to windshields, record each vehicle's plate and tare, create user accounts, and enrol everyone in MFA.

  11. Parallel run weeks 13–15

    • Two weeks of the manual register alongside the automatic system. Reconcile every day and tune the tare limits and stability window.

  12. Security test and go-live weeks 15–18

    • Security audit, fix findings, go live. Train operators and supervisors on one-page SOPs, then three weeks of close support.

13 · Before 2 October

Questions to put to Essae before ordering

  1. Platform size, capacity, number of load cells and scale interval

    "Oxygen Series 1600P" isn't explained, and the load cells are quoted as 18.2 t or 34 t without saying which.

  2. Indicator output

    Will they share the IWT-186 data string format and enable TCP output? The brochure lists a 10,000-count analog load-cell module (5 V excitation) while the quote is for digital cells. How do the two connect?

  3. Open access to the AWS hardware

    Admin credentials for the cameras, RFID reader and switch. The reader's model and LLRP support. Can the hardware be bought without their software, and at what price?

  4. Database and export

    MySQL or MSSQL? Read access to the tables, and the price of the "optional" JSON web report and SAP/ERP connector.

  5. Scope conflicts

    One boom barrier or two? Who supplies the printer and UPS: page 16 says Essae, page 17 says you.

  6. Service

    AMC price after year 1, response time in Chandrapur, spares held locally, and how their remote support connects to your system (it must go through your VPN).

  7. Operating temperature and security

    Do they require an air-conditioned control room, given the 40 °C limit? Does their software support MFA, per-user audit trails and records that can't be edited?
